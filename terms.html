Fair use and limits

The subscription's AI costs us real money with every request, so it has limits. They're there to keep it working for everyone, and they never cost you extra.

  • Each account has a monthly allowance of AI use [to confirm how much, and how it's shown]. When it's used up, Pip tells you, and the subscription's AI stops until next month. There are no surprise charges, and you can switch to your own AI meanwhile.
  • There's a limit on how many requests one person can make a minute and a day, and on how much one person can use in a day.
  • While Pip is in early access, the subscription's AI also has a daily and a weekly budget shared by everyone. If the day's runs out, it pauses for everyone until midnight UTC. If the week's runs out, it pauses until Monday at midnight UTC. Pip tells you when it'll be back.
  • Only so many new people can start the subscription each day.

While it's paused, your work is all there, and your own AI keeps working.

Your work stays yours

Everything you make in Pip is yours: notes, tables, decks, photos and canvases, and what Pip writes or makes for you in them. We claim no rights in any of it.

It's saved in a folder on your Mac, in ordinary files you can open in other apps. We don't receive it, so we don't need a licence to it. On the Pip subscription, your requests pass through our server only to get you an answer. We use them for that and nothing else, and we don't keep them.

Make sure you have the right to what you bring in, such as other people's writing or photos of other people.

Because your work lives on your Mac, keep a backup, with Time Machine or a synced folder. We can't recover it for you.

AI can be wrong

Pip's AI is useful, and it makes mistakes. It can misread your work, miss something, or say something untrue with confidence. Check what matters before you rely on it: numbers, dates, names, facts about people, and anything legal, medical or financial.

Pip works from your notes and shows you which ones, so you can check. He isn't a lawyer, accountant, doctor or any other adviser, and what he writes isn't advice.

You decide what to send, publish or act on. Pip doesn't send email or post messages for you, and to begin with he asks before changing anything outside your Mac, like a Notion page.
