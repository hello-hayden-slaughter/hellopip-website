Privacy policy.

What Pip collects, who handles it, how long it's kept, and what you can ask for.

The short version

  • Your work is saved in a folder on your Mac. We never receive it.
  • When you ask Pip's AI for help, what that job needs goes to the AI you chose, and the answer comes back to your Mac.
  • With the Pip subscription, your request passes through our server in Frankfurt on its way to the AI. It isn't stored or logged.
  • Your Pip account knows who you are and how you get your AI. Never what you write.
  • This website has no cookies, no analytics and no trackers.
  • To see, correct or delete what we hold about you, email hello@haydenslaughter.co.uk.

This website

hellopip.co.uk is a plain website. It sets no cookies and runs no analytics, trackers or adverts. Its fonts are served from the site itself, not from Google or anyone else.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub sees your IP address and the pages your browser asks for, and may keep them in its logs for security and to keep the service running. We don't see or use those logs. GitHub's own privacy statement covers them. [to confirm]

When your place is ready, you'll download Pip from GitHub, which shows GitHub your IP address. The sign-up page itself doesn't contact GitHub.

The sign-up form

While Pip is invite-only, Download for Mac leads to a short form for a place in the first 50 free trials. It asks for:

  • your name;
  • your email address;
  • what you do, if you'd like to say. You can leave it blank.

We use your name and email to send you your trial. If you tell us what you do, it helps us understand who Pip is for. Hayden reads each one and writes to you himself. You won't be added to a mailing list, and we won't send you marketing. Ask to be removed at any time and we'll delete your answers.

The answers are stored in Pip's Supabase project, on servers in Frankfurt. [to confirm]

The app on your Mac

Pip saves your notes, tables, decks, photos and canvases as files in a folder on your Mac. Pip also keeps its own working files on your Mac: what Pip remembers about you and your work, your chats, your settings, a search index and its logs. Your API keys and sign-in details are kept in an encrypted file on your Mac.

We don't receive any of it. There's no copy on a server of ours. Pip sends no analytics, no crash reports and no usage statistics: there's no code in Pip that does.

Because it's on your Mac, it's yours to manage. Back it up, move it, open it in other apps or delete it. If you delete it, we can't bring it back.

Two things worth knowing. The key that unlocks your encrypted file sits on the same Mac, so it's only as safe as your Mac's own login. And Pip keeps its working files in a folder that other accounts on the same Mac can reach. If you share your Mac, keep that in mind. [to confirm]

What Pip fetches on its own

A few requests happen without you connecting anything. None carries your work, but like any download they show your IP address to the host:

  • on a fresh install, a small model from Hugging Face that Pip uses to find related things in your work. It then runs on your Mac;
  • the first time you use Clean up in the image editor, its model, from Hugging Face;
  • each time Pip starts, a check with GitHub for a newer version, which sends the version you have. Nothing downloads until you say yes.

When you ask Pip's AI

When you ask Pip for help, Pip sends what that job needs to the AI you chose: your message, the parts of your work the job uses (a note, a table, a picture you show Pip), the conversation so far, and Pip's own instructions. The answer comes back to your Mac and is saved there.

Pip also uses AI for small jobs in the background, such as summing up a long conversation or keeping track of its subjects. These go to the AI you chose for background work, which can be a different one. You choose both in Settings.

Where it goes depends on that choice.

With the Pip subscription

Your request goes from your Mac to Pip's relay, a small server function in Frankfurt, run on Supabase. The relay checks you're signed in and within your allowance, passes the request on to EU Router with our key, and sends the answer back. EU Router passes it to the company that runs the model: Amazon Web Services (AWS Bedrock) for the Claude models, in Amazon's EU regions, and Lyceum (Germany), Inceptron (Sweden) or Tensorix (Ireland) for GLM-5.3 Flash. EU Router's agreement with us keeps all of this inside the EU.

What you write, and any picture you show Pip, passes through the relay and is never stored or logged there. A picture goes as a smaller copy. The relay keeps only who made the request, the model, whether it worked and what it cost.

We don't use what you write to train AI. EU Router doesn't either: its data processing agreement says so, and it doesn't keep your words once it has answered. It lists each company that runs these models as keeping nothing and training on nothing.

With your own Claude plan or API key

Your request goes straight from your Mac to that provider, under your own account. It doesn't pass through us and we never see it. That provider's terms and privacy policy apply, including what it keeps and whether it trains on your data. Pip works with Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI, OpenRouter, GreenPT and DeepSeek. OpenRouter passes your request on to the company that runs the model you pick.

On your Claude plan, Pip runs Anthropic's own Claude Code on your Mac, signed in to your Claude account. You sign in through Anthropic, and Pip never sees your password. Claude Code keeps its own record of each chat on your Mac, which Pip's delete doesn't reach, and includes your Claude account's email address in what it sends to Anthropic.

With a model on your Mac

If your main and background AI both run on your Mac (with Ollama), nothing you ask leaves it. If you point Ollama at another computer, your requests go there instead.

Searching the web

Internet access is off until you switch it on. When it's on and Pip searches, only the search words go out: to Anthropic or to DuckDuckGo, depending on your AI. On the Pip subscription, searches go to DuckDuckGo. When Pip reads a page, it fetches it from that website like a browser would, without your cookies or logins.

Services you connect

If you connect Slack, Notion, Gmail, Google Calendar or Granola, Pip reads from them on your Mac with your own sign-in, when a job needs it. What it reads may then go to your AI as part of that job. Pip never sends email or Slack messages, and only writes to Notion when you allow it. These connections are between you and that service. We don't receive anything from them.

Your Pip account

Pip asks you to sign in with Apple when you set it up. You need an account for the Pip subscription and pictures. You can carry on without one and use your own AI.

Sign in with Apple happens in your browser, and Pip never sees your Apple password. Apple gives us your email address and a user id, and Pip asks for your name. If you choose Hide My Email, we get Apple's forwarding address instead of yours.

What the account holds

It's kept in Pip's Supabase project, on servers in Frankfurt:

  • your email address (or Apple's forwarding address), your name and the user id Apple gives us;
  • a one-way fingerprint of that Apple user id, so your allowance stays with your Apple ID if you sign in again;
  • whether you chose the Pip subscription or your own AI, the history of each change and why it happened, and which provider your main and background AI use (for example Anthropic, or a model on your Mac). Never your keys;
  • your monthly allowance, when you started the subscription, and what you've used each month, as a cost and a number of requests;
  • counts that keep the limits working, such as how many requests you've made this minute and today;
  • when Pip last checked in (once each time it starts), and when your account was made and last changed;
  • for each sign-in, when it happened, your IP address and the app or browser it came from. Supabase also logs each sign-in, sign-out and refresh with your IP address and email;
  • one log line for each AI or picture request: your account id, the model, whether it worked and the cost. Never what you wrote;
  • if you were invited, whether you came in through the invite list or a code. We keep the list of invited email addresses and the codes we've made, with who used each.

It never holds your notes, chats, files or memory, anything you type to Pip or anything Pip replies, or your own API keys and Claude sign-in. Those stay on your Mac.

On your Mac, Pip keeps your sign-in session in the encrypted file. Signing out removes it.

If you sign in with Apple but aren't on the invite list, Supabase still keeps your sign-in (your email or Apple's forwarding address, and your Apple user id) so we can let you in later. No account record is made and no use is recorded, apart from a count of how often your sign-in asks to join, which stops people guessing invite codes.

Pictures

With the Pip subscription, Pip can make a picture from a description, or change a photo you describe ("take the people out", "make it evening"). Nothing is sent unless you ask for a picture, and to begin with Pip checks with you before it makes one.

The description, and for a change the photo (made smaller first), go from your Mac through Pip's pictures function in Frankfurt to Black Forest Labs' EU service, which makes the picture. Black Forest Labs runs on Microsoft Azure. The picture comes back and is saved in your folder.

Our server doesn't keep the words or the pictures. It records only who asked, the kind of change, the model, whether it worked and the cost. Our Black Forest Labs account is set so they don't use what we send, or the pictures they make, to train their models, and don't keep them after the picture comes back. That's a setting on our account. A signed data processing agreement has been asked for and isn't in place yet. [to confirm]

You can turn pictures off in Settings, under Models and usage. The image editor's own tools, Clean up included, work on your Mac and send nothing anywhere.

Emails to us

If you write to hello@haydenslaughter.co.uk, we get your email address, what you write and anything you attach. We use it to reply and to help. Our email is handled by [to confirm].

If something goes wrong, Pip can save a diagnostics file for you to send. It holds Pip's logs and the versions of Pip and macOS. The logs can include short extracts of what Pip's tools returned, so look through it before you send it. Nothing is sent unless you send it.

Outside the UK and EU

Pip's server is in Frankfurt, and the UK recognises the EU as protecting personal data properly. Some of the companies we use are based in the US: GitHub, Apple, Supabase [to confirm] and Amazon. Amazon runs Pip's Claude requests in its EU regions, so they stay in the EU. Where data reaches the US, we rely on [to confirm with a lawyer] to protect it.

Some services you can choose yourself are outside the UK and EU: Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Slack, Notion, Google and DuckDuckGo are in the US, and DeepSeek is in China. Sending your work there is your choice, under your agreement with them. If you'd rather it went through EU servers, use the Pip subscription, and if you'd rather it never left your Mac, use a model on your Mac.

Your rights

You can ask us:

  • for a copy of the personal data we hold about you;
  • to correct it if it's wrong;
  • to delete it;
  • to stop using it, or to use it only in limited ways, and to object to how we use it;
  • for it in a file you can take elsewhere;
  • to withdraw your consent, where that's our reason.

Email hello@haydenslaughter.co.uk, from the address on your account if you have one, so we know it's you. It's free, and we'll reply within a month.

Your work on your Mac is already yours. You don't need to ask us for it.

Complaints

If you're unhappy with how we've handled your data, tell us first and we'll try to put it right. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK regulator, at ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint or on 0303 123 1113. If you live in the EU, you can complain to the data protection authority where you live.

Deleting your account

There's no delete button yet. To delete your account, email hello@haydenslaughter.co.uk from the address on the account. Hayden deletes it himself and tells you when it's done, within [to confirm].

What goes: your sign-in, with your email address and name. Your account record is unlinked from it.

What stays today: the rest of your account record (the Apple ID fingerprint, your allowance, your choices of AI and their history, the providers and the dates), what you used each month, and the limit counts. None of it carries your name or email. But the fingerprint stays the same for your Apple ID: if it signs in again, it gets the old record back. That's so a new account can't be used to get a fresh allowance. Supabase's own sign-in log, request logs and backups keep what they hold until they expire. [to confirm]

[to confirm with a lawyer] Whether we may keep the fingerprint, the monthly use and the history for ever after someone asks for their data to be deleted is an open question under UK GDPR. Until it's answered, this policy says what happens, not that it's right.

Signing out ends your session on that Mac only. It doesn't delete your account.

Your work and Pip's own files on your Mac are yours to delete whenever you like.

Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. If a change matters, such as a new company handling your data, we'll email people with an account before it applies. [to confirm]

Questions about any of it? Write to hello@haydenslaughter.co.uk. Hayden reads every email.
